# PROJECT DUEL design

The training facility is a symmetric 40 × 40 arena with dark metallic cover, cyan navigation strips, violet accents and four floor-standing robot targets at the north end. The robots share the multiplayer model, match the 1.7-meter player capsule and occupy clear firing lanes without raised platforms. Shared solid geometry supplies both the client scene and authoritative server physics. Training has amber hazard pads; multiplayer replaces targets/hazards with a second player and opposite spawns.

The practice drone and Sparring entry are removed. Stationary training robots and multiplayer opponents use the same procedural model and body proportions, defined as shared boxes. Head hits deal 2× base damage, torso 1×, arms/legs 0.75× and feet 0.5×. Rendered parts carry region labels for offline raycasts; the server intersects matching rotated boxes and independently validates online hits. Cover blocks each ray. EMP is flat area damage without regional scaling. Training robots have 100 HP, award 100 points once, fold down when defeated, and reset after three seconds. Platforms keep torso/head practice visible above low cover; foot practice requires an unobstructed lane.

Four weapons share tuning across offline/server code: a semi-automatic 12-round energy pistol; a 60-base-damage blade with 2.6-unit reach; a 30-round automatic rifle; and a six-shell shotgun with eight deterministic spread rays and distance falloff. Actual damage is capped by remaining health. Headshots have coral feedback and an explicit label; eliminations show ELIMINATED. Shotguns use a dotted circle without a center plus.

Weapon switching takes 0.25 seconds and preserves separate magazines. Shared recovery prevents weapon switching from bypassing cooldowns. Blade swings must finish before switching. Empty firearms auto reload; manual reload handles partial magazines. A weapon tilt/cell exchange, notice, progress bar and countdown communicate reloads. Switching cancels incomplete reloads, and empty re-equipped firearms restart after equip recovery. Training pauses all timers on Escape/focus loss.

Training abilities are Dash, Shield, Scanner and EMP. Dash sweeps through physical collision; Shield absorbs hazard damage; Scanner reveals robot markers through cover; EMP deals nearby flat damage, jams robots and disables pads. All four abilities now work online with server-owned activation, cooldowns and effects. Shield absorbs weapon damage. EMP strips shields, deals 40 damage and disables abilities for four seconds without disabling weapons or ordinary movement. Online cooldowns persist across respawns. See [tactical systems](TACTICAL_SYSTEMS.md).

Private rooms support two players, a shared countdown, server-owned 60 Hz Rapier movement, 20 Hz snapshots, remote interpolation, four validated weapons, health, kills/deaths and three-second respawns. Combat intents contain aim/actions, never positions, hit results or damage. A disconnect pauses shared combat/movement/timers for the reconnect window. Returning to lobby disposes the world and readying again resets it. The host selects Best of Three (default) or Free Play. Best of Three uses first-to-two round wins, 90-second health-tiebreak rounds, draw replays, four-second results, three-second countdowns and complete round resets. Scores, forfeits and match winners come from the server. Free Play keeps three-second respawns. Results persist in memory until lobby return, where both players can ready up for a new match. See [authority and limits](ONLINE_ARENA.md).

All assets use procedural geometry and system fonts. No external model, image, font service, or audio asset is required. UI, rendering, input, local rules, shared geometry/tuning, networking and authoritative simulation are separate modules. Server state drives online weapon animation and feedback without changing offline training state. High-latency prediction/rewind and hands-on GPU/performance testing remain.
